How scoring works

ResuMatch uses a rule-based engine. No AI model, no third-party API. Every point in the score traces to a rule in a single file: AtsWeights.java. This page documents the exact formula.

01 · The general ATS score

A resume uploaded by a seeker gets a general score out of 100, made of five sub-scores. Each sub-score has a maximum, and the engine sums them up.

Sub-scoreMaxPointsHow it is computed
Formatting2020Deductions for each formatting flag. Missing email/phone/sections cost up to 5 points each.
Completeness1515Email present (+5), phone (+4), name (+3), summary section (+3).
Structure2020Up to 6 standard sections detected (summary, skills, experience, education, projects, certifications).
Skills2525Distinct canonical skills found, capped at 14. Penalty if no skills section.
Experience2020Years estimated from date ranges, plus presence of projects section.

The total is the sum of the five sub-scores, capped at 100. A perfect resume scores 100.

02 · The company-specific score

When a seeker applies to a job, the engine computes a second score using the job's weighted skill rows. The recruiter defines each skill's importance and weight when posting the job.

ComponentMaxPointsHow it is computed
Must-have skills5050sum(weight of matched must-haves) / sum(weight of all must-haves) × 50
Nice-to-have skills2020Same formula applied to nice-to-haves.
Experience1515Years of experience, capped at 5 years.
Education55Degree keyword + section presence.
Resume health1010Formatting deductions; capped lower if the general score is poor.

Worked example

A job asking for Java (must, weight 10), Spring Boot (must, weight 8), MySQL (must, weight 6), Docker (nice, weight 3) — a resume that mentions Java + Spring Boot + MySQL:

Must-have earned10 + 8 + 6 = 24
Must-have total10 + 8 + 6 = 24
Must-have score50 / 50
+ Nice-to-have0 / 20
+ Experience, education, formatting28 / 30
Total78 / 100

The candidate sees the same number the recruiter does. The same formula. The same breakdown. Nothing hidden.

03 · Alias matching

The engine ships with 50+ canonical skills and 200+ aliases. reactjs, react.js, and React all resolve to React; node js, nodejs, and node.js all resolve to Node.js. The matcher uses a longest-first greedy scan so multi-word skills like "Spring Boot" don't get matched as "Spring".

04 · What we don't do

  • We do not call any external API. No OpenAI, no Google, no third-party ATS.
  • We do not train or fine-tune a model. There is no model.
  • We do not share resumes with anyone. The recruiter only sees what the seeker applied with.
  • We do not surface a score to the recruiter that the seeker cannot also see.
